## Estimating the carbon impacts of leakage from forest restoration and the costs of reducing them

Together with colleagues, I investigated how forest restoration can displace economic
activities and trigger leakage, whereby forgone production drives habitat loss elsewhere.
Focusing on reforestation opportunities in Brazilian ranchland, we characterized leakage
risk as the ratio of forgone beef production to carbon gained.

We assessed the impacts of unaddressed leakage, the benefits of prioritizing restoration
in low-yielding, high-carbon areas, and the cost-effectiveness of targeted intensification
to reduce leakage. The results show that leakage can substantially increase the carbon
costs of restoration, while careful project siting and targeted intensification can provide
cost-effective mitigation.
